\begin{abstract}
Bayesian sequential estimation (BSE) detects hallucinated claims in LLM output by
retrieving evidence documents one at a time and stopping once the posterior is
decisive. Every document costs a retrieval and several NLI calls, so the
evidence model that turns a document into a likelihood ratio decides how much
the detector costs. We show that BSE's Bayes-risk look-ahead is exactly a
posterior band, so detectors can be compared on a common stopping rule and
differ only in their evidence model. We then replace BSE's one-dimensional
entailment histogram with a self-normalised uLSIF density ratio over three
document features: entailment, contradiction and retrieval rank. In an
exploratory comparison on three test sets, this multivariate evidence model
matched BSE's balanced PR-AUC with 3.21 fewer documents per sentence
(95\% CI [2.90, 3.55]) and improved balanced PR-AUC by 0.096 [0.037, 0.158] at
matched cost on Factcheck-Bench. The gain was absent on SelfCheckGPT and
reversed on FActScore. Along the way we found that a cost--quality sweep
containing stopping bands that exclude the prior produces a zero-document
detector whose PR-AUC beats every real detector, and we report the corrected
analysis. We then ran a preregistered confirmatory test on ExpertQA and
BingCheck: \pending{confirmatory outcome}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-based hallucination detectors decompose a generated sentence into
claims, retrieve evidence for each claim and score claim--evidence pairs with an
NLI model \citep{min-etal-2023-factscore,manakul-etal-2023-selfcheckgpt,
wang-etal-2024-factcheck}. Their cost grows with the number of documents read.
\citet{wang-etal-2023-hallucination} cast detection as Bayesian sequential
estimation (BSE): documents are read one at a time, a naive-Bayes likelihood
ratio updates the posterior that a claim is factual, and a Bayes-risk
look-ahead decides whether another document is worth its cost
\citep{wald1945sequential}. BSE reads fewer documents than fixed-$k$ retrieval.

This paper asks whether a better \emph{evidence model}, the map from one
document to a likelihood ratio, can make BSE cheaper. Our research questions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1 (cost).} At BSE's detection quality, does a density-ratio
evidence model read fewer documents and make fewer NLI calls?
\item \textbf{RQ2 (quality).} At BSE's cost, does it detect hallucinations
better?
\item \textbf{RQ3 (components).} Is any difference associated with more training
data, ratio normalisation, or richer document features?
\end{itemize}

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We show that BSE's look-ahead, at its published costs, continues exactly
while the posterior lies in a fixed interval (0.204, 0.769). Every detector can
therefore share one stopping rule and be compared on its evidence model alone
(\S\ref{sec:band}).
\item We propose \emph{multivariate DDRE}: direct density-ratio estimation
\citep{kanamori2009ulsif,sugiyama2012density} over entailment, contradiction
and log rank, self-normalised so the ratio averages one under the
hallucinated-claim distribution (\S\ref{sec:method}).
\item We report a staged evaluation in the order it happened. A one-dimensional
DDRE saved 20.7\% of documents on SelfCheckGPT but not on FActScore. The
multivariate model gave a large gain on Factcheck-Bench, no clear gain on
SelfCheckGPT and a loss on FActScore (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\item We document a pitfall in cost--quality curves for sequential detectors:
a stopping band that excludes the prior yields a constant, zero-cost detector
whose PR-AUC under a common implementation is $(1+\pi)/2$
(\S\ref{sec:pitfall}).
\item We preregistered \citep{nosek2018preregistration} and ran a confirmatory
test on two untouched datasets, ExpertQA \citep{malaviya-etal-2024-expertqa}
and BingCheck \citep{li-etal-2024-self} (\S\ref{sec:confirmatory}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background: Bayesian sequential estimation}
\label{sec:bse}

For a claim $c$ and its ranked documents $d_1, d_2, \dots$, an NLI model gives
each document a score $s_j \in [0, 100]$: the maximum entailment probability
over 400-word spans, with temperature 5 \citep{wang-etal-2023-hallucination}.
BSE discretises $s_j$ into ten buckets and estimates
$P(b \mid \text{factual})$ and $P(b \mid \text{hallucinated})$ from labelled
pairs with Laplace smoothing. Starting from prior $P_0 = 0.5$, the posterior
after $j$ documents is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{logit} P_j = \operatorname{logit} P_{j-1}
  + \log \frac{P(b_j \mid \text{factual})}{P(b_j \mid \text{hallucinated})}.
\end{equation}
BSE reads another document only if the expected Bayes risk after one more
document, plus the retrieval cost, is below the risk of stopping now, with miss
cost $C_M$, false-alarm cost $C_{FA}$ and retrieval cost 1. A sentence's
posterior is the minimum over its claims, and it is labelled factual iff
$P > C_M / (C_M + C_{FA})$.

\paragraph{The look-ahead is a posterior band.}
\label{sec:band}
With the histogram fixed, the look-ahead decision depends only on the current
$P$. We computed the set where it continues by bisection and checked it on a
grid of 20{,}001 points. At $C_M = 28$, $C_{FA} = 96$ it is exactly the
interval $(0.20404, 0.76895)$. Replaying BSE as ``continue while $P$ is in
this band'' reproduces its document choices on all 2{,}387 SelfCheckGPT
held-out claims with zero mismatches, and on every test set of \S\ref{sec:results} (the replay checks it again on each new set). So
any evidence model can be run under BSE's own stopping rule, and differences
between detectors come from the evidence model alone.

\section{Multivariate density-ratio evidence}
\label{sec:method}

BSE's update needs only the likelihood ratio
$r(x) = p(x \mid \text{factual}) / p(x \mid \text{hallucinated})$ of a
document's features $x$. Instead of estimating two densities and dividing, we
estimate $r$ directly.

\paragraph{Features.} For each document: $e$, the maximum entailment over spans
(BSE's score); $k$, the maximum contradiction over spans, from the same NLI
forward pass; and $\log(\text{rank})$. Adding $k$ and the rank costs no extra
NLI calls.

\paragraph{Estimator.} We fit unconstrained least-squares importance fitting
(uLSIF; \citealp{kanamori2009ulsif}) with 100 Gaussian kernel centres on
standardised features. The kernel width (0.5, 1, 2 or 4 times the median
pairwise distance) and the ridge penalty ($10^{-4}$ to $10$) are chosen only by
uLSIF's own 5-fold cross-validation objective, never by any detection metric.
uLSIF's fitted ratio need not average one under the denominator distribution.
In an earlier one-dimensional fit, the mean ratio over hallucinated-claim
documents ranged from 0.63 to 74.8 across hyperparameters, which biases every
update. We therefore self-normalise: $\hat r \leftarrow \hat r /
\overline{\hat r}_{h}$, where $\overline{\hat r}_{h}$ is the mean over the
training documents of hallucinated claims.

\paragraph{Cells.} To separate the components (RQ3) we compare five evidence
models under the same band:
\textbf{A}, BSE's histogram fit on Wang's data;
\textbf{A$'$}, the same histogram refit on our training data;
\textbf{U}, normalised uLSIF on $e$ alone;
\textbf{M}, normalised uLSIF on $(e, k, \log \text{rank})$;
\textbf{L}, a logistic-regression density ratio on the same three features
\citep{bickel2009discriminative}.

\paragraph{Training data.} An entity-disjoint split of FActScore
\citep{min-etal-2023-factscore}: 91 of 182 entities, chosen by the order of
the SHA-256 hash of their names. That gives 7{,}382 human-labelled facts, each
with ten BM25-retrieved Wikipedia passages: 52{,}360 documents of factual facts
and 21{,}460 of hallucinated ones.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{NLI model.} Wang's released checkpoint
(\texttt{MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-large-mnli-fever-anli-ling-wanli};
\citealp{he2023debertav3,laurer2024less}) in float16, with Wang's segmentation.
Before every scoring run, the 398 pairs behind Wang's histogram must reproduce
earlier caches bit for bit.

\paragraph{Reproduction.} Our BSE reproduction matches Wang's Table~1 at
$C_M{=}28$, $C_{FA}{=}96$ on all six metrics within the predeclared tolerances
(e.g.\ nonfactual PR-AUC 0.8595 vs 0.8645, 6.47 vs 6.22 documents per
sentence). At $C_M{=}14$, $C_{FA}{=}24$ it reads 16\% more documents than
reported and has higher correlations. Loading the checkpoint in float32
changes every metric by at most 0.001, so precision does not explain the
gap. All comparisons in this paper use the $28/96$ operating point.

\paragraph{Cost--quality curves.} Each cell runs under BSE's band $(l^*, u^*)$
and under scaled bands
$(\sigma(s \cdot \operatorname{logit} l^*), \sigma(s \cdot \operatorname{logit} u^*))$
for $s = 0.25, 0.50, \dots, 4$. Every scaled band contains $P_0$, and $s = 1$
is BSE's band. Quality is balanced PR-AUC: the mean of factual and nonfactual
PR-AUC, computed as in Wang's code. Cost is documents (and NLI calls) per
sentence.

\paragraph{Endpoints and statistics.} The matched-cost gain (RQ2) is a cell's
balanced PR-AUC at BSE's documents per sentence, interpolated on its curve,
minus BSE's. The matched-quality saving (RQ1) is the reduction in documents at
BSE's balanced PR-AUC. All endpoints come from a paired cluster bootstrap
(10{,}000 resamples, seed 42) over responses, passages or entities, with every
cell evaluated on the same draws.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{One-dimensional DDRE}

A first, one-dimensional uLSIF on $e$ was selected on SelfCheckGPT validation
data (640 configurations) and frozen. On the SelfCheckGPT held-out split (190
passages, 1{,}525 sentences) it read 20.7\% fewer documents than BSE
(1.32 per sentence, [1.15, 1.49]) and made 22.0\% fewer NLI calls, with balanced
PR-AUC +0.008 [$-0.021$, 0.038]. Because the configuration was chosen from many
candidates, we treated this as non-confirmatory and preregistered a frozen
transfer to FActScore. There the same detector kept BSE's quality (balanced
PR-AUC $-0.002$ [$-0.028$, 0.023]) but read 9.4\% \emph{more} documents. The
saving had not transferred.

Two facts explained the reversal. First, the histogram and the 1-D uLSIF
estimate the same function, $r(e)$, so their costs depend on where each
dataset's scores fall. Second, the frozen uLSIF was unnormalised (mean ratio
0.736 under the hallucinated distribution), which biased its updates toward
``hallucinated''. This motivated normalisation and additional features.

On Factcheck-Bench, M's curve lies above every other cell's at nearly every
cost. On SelfCheckGPT the curves lie within a few hundredths of one another.
On FActScore, BSE's official run lies above its own neighbouring curve points
(0.763 vs 0.680 and 0.662), and the negative matched-cost value is measured
against that single point.

\paragraph{The same band.} Under BSE's own band, every new cell reads
\emph{more} documents than BSE on all three test sets (M: 0.79, 1.36 and 9.22
more per sentence). Better evidence does not by itself shorten the walk to a
fixed band. The savings in Table~\ref{tab:explore} come from moving along the
cost--quality curve to a tighter band.

\paragraph{Components (RQ3).} On Factcheck-Bench, the matched-cost difference
between M and U (adding $k$ and the rank) is +0.088 [0.036, 0.143], and between
M and L (uLSIF vs logistic on the same features) is +0.070 [0.007, 0.133]. More
training data (A$\to$A$'$: +0.035 [$-$0.010, 0.078]) and normalisation
(A$'\to$U: $-$0.028 [$-$0.069, 0.019]) show no consistent difference. M also
exceeds L on SelfCheckGPT (+0.023 [0.001, 0.046]). These are associations within
our design, not isolated causes.

\subsection{A pitfall in cost--quality curves}
\label{sec:pitfall}

Our preregistered curve swept bands of half-width 0.25 to 4 in log-odds around
the decision threshold $\operatorname{logit}(28/124) = -1.23$. For half-widths
up to 1, the whole band lies below $P_0 = 0.5$, so the detector reads no
document and outputs $P = 0.5$ for every sentence. The common PR-AUC
implementation (\texttt{precision\_recall\_curve} followed by trapezoidal
\texttt{auc}) gives a constant score the value $(1 + \pi)/2$ for positive
prevalence $\pi$, so balanced PR-AUC is exactly 0.75. That beat every real
detector on Factcheck-Bench (BSE: 0.609). Matched endpoints then interpolated
to this point: every cell, including BSE itself, appeared to save all of BSE's
documents. We kept the first output frozen, recorded a dated amendment, and
replaced the sweep by the scaled bands of \S\ref{sec:protocol}, which always
contain $P_0$. All multivariate results above are therefore exploratory.

A second, subtler optimism remains: on a noisy curve, the first crossing of a
quality target can occur early by chance. BSE's own curve ``saves'' 2.48
documents against BSE's official run on Factcheck-Bench by this rule. The
confirmatory test corrects for it.

\subsection{Preregistered confirmatory test}
\label{sec:confirmatory}

Before any model touched them, we preregistered a test of M against BSE on two
datasets with the same structure (LLM answers, claim-level human labels, saved
ranked evidence):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ExpertQA} \citep{malaviya-etal-2024-expertqa}: 11{,}389
expert-labelled sentences (596 hallucinated) in 483 question-author clusters.
Each atomic claim has five released passages.
\item \textbf{BingCheck} \citep{li-etal-2024-self}: 3{,}623 claims (942
refuted or partially supported) in 396 responses. Each claim has three saved
Bing pages.
\end{itemize}

The endpoints are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{E2}, the matched-cost gain.
\item \textbf{E1}, a \emph{net} matched-quality saving. It compares M's first
crossing of BSE's quality with BSE's own first crossing, capped at BSE's cost.
\end{itemize}
Holm's correction \citep{holm1979simple} runs over the four tests. A
hypothesis counts as confirmed only if supported on both datasets.

\section{Discussion}

\pending{To be written after the confirmatory result. Points to cover:}
\begin{itemize}
\item The band equivalence makes the evidence model the only lever on BSE's
cost, and the curve, not the fixed band, is where the savings appear.
\item Contradiction and rank carry information that the entailment histogram
discards, at no extra NLI cost.
\item Dataset dependence: Factcheck-Bench vs SelfCheckGPT and FActScore;
\pending{confirmatory pattern}.
\item A practical rule for evaluating sequential detectors: every band in a
sweep must contain the prior.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exploratory status.} The multivariate results are exploratory,
and Factcheck-Bench is small: 271 sentences, at most 5 documents per claim.
\item \textbf{Grid edge.} Cross-validation chose the smallest kernel width on
the fixed grid for U and M.
\item \textbf{Label semantics.} ExpertQA has one annotator per sentence and few
hallucinations. BingCheck labels measure support by the saved pages, not
world-level truth.
\item \textbf{Reproduction gap.} Our BSE reproduction fails Wang's
$C_M{=}14$, $C_{FA}{=}24$ row, so we use only the 28/96 operating point.
\item \textbf{Cost measure.} Cost is counted in documents and NLI calls.
Wall-clock time is \pending{to be measured}.
\end{itemize}
